A light left on, and a chair kept for you.

Lighthouses are built for one job: to be the thing you steer towards. Ours is a room, not a tower — but the idea holds.

The floor is the first thing people notice. Hand-laid monochrome tile at the entrance, worn smooth where the door swings. Inside it gives way to warm stone, walnut tables, a green back wall and a window the plants have taken over.

The coffee is single origin, roasted light, and changed when something better lands. Espresso goes through a two-group lever; filter is brewed to order on the bar. Everything in the kitchen is cooked the same morning — the mushroom toast is the one people come back for.

Breakfast has no closing time here.

Eggs at four in the afternoon are not a mistake, they are a decision. The kitchen runs the full menu from the moment the shutter goes up until a quarter past ten at night.

Bread is baked in-house each morning and the pastry case is restocked once, at noon, until it is empty. Most of the menu can be made vegan; ask whoever brings your water.
